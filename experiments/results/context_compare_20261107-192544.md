**Short answer:** The evidence supports the debit/checking side (Reg E) in detail. It does not give the credit card liability cap or the Reg Z notice deadline, and the complaint data doesn't isolate card or checking fraud.

**Debit card / checking (Reg E, 12 CFR 1005.6):**
- Liability applies only if the institution gave the required 1005.7(b)(1)-(3) disclosures. For an access device, it must be an accepted device and the institution must have provided a means to identify the user [R-1005.6#1].
- If the consumer notifies within two business days after learning of the loss or theft, liability is the lesser of $50 or the unauthorized transfers made before notice [R-1005.6#1].
- If not, liability is capped at the lesser of $500 or the sum of the amounts in 1005.6(b)(2)(i)-(ii). Under (ii), later transfers count only if the institution establishes they would not have occurred with timely notice [R-1005.6#1].
- Unauthorized transfers on a periodic statement must be reported within 60 days of transmittal to avoid liability for later transfers [R-1005.6#1].
- Extenuating circumstances require a reasonable extension [R-1005.6#1].
- Notice may be oral or written [R-1005.6#1].
- A lower limit under state law or agreement controls [R-1005.6#2].

**$700 card purchases (Reg Z):** The evidence covers only billing-error procedures, 1026.13(d)-(g). While a dispute is pending, the consumer need not pay the disputed amount, and the creditor may not collect it or make adverse credit reports over it [R-1026.13#2].
