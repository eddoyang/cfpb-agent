**Short answer:** The evidence supports the Regulation E limits for the unauthorized electronic fund transfers. It does not include the credit card liability rules, so I can't state those limits.

**Regulation E (electronic fund transfers, 12 CFR 1005.6):**
- Liability applies only if the institution gave the required disclosures. If an access device was involved, it must be an accepted access device and the institution must have provided a means to identify the consumer. [R-1005.6#1]
- Notice within two business days of learning of the loss or theft: liability is capped at the lesser of $50 or the unauthorized transfers made before notice. [R-1005.6#1]
- No notice within two days: liability is capped at the lesser of $500 or a sum defined in the rule. Transfers after day two count only if the institution establishes they would not have occurred with timely notice. [R-1005.6#1]
- A consumer must report an unauthorized transfer shown on a periodic statement within 60 days of the statement's transmittal to avoid liability for later transfers. [R-1005.6#1]
- Delay caused by extenuating circumstances requires a reasonable extension. [R-1005.6#1]
- If state law or the account agreement imposes less liability, that lower amount governs. [R-1005.6#2]
